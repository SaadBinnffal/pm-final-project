Problem Hook & Value Proposition: RouteLogic Velocity

Module 1 · Deliverable 1

1. Chosen scenario

Path: RouteLogic Velocity (B2B).

I chose RouteLogic because it presents a clear tension between enterprise breadth and frontline usability: the product must make everyday coordination simpler while preserving the advanced capabilities enterprise customers still need.

2. The strategic crisis

If we do nothing for 12 months, RouteLogic risks losing its largest enterprise account and weakening its position against simpler competitors. Declining frontline usage and the account's competitor trial suggest that expanding feature breadth is no longer sufficient to protect customer value or renewals.

These are risks inferred from the scenario, not a quantified revenue forecast.

3. The moment of misery

During time-sensitive fleet coordination, the frontline user has to navigate administrative complexity that slows the operational task they came to complete.

Proposed research focus: Reassigning a delivery when a driver becomes unavailable. We will examine the steps, interruptions, and information needed to complete this task correctly. This specific task is a working focus for discovery, not a finding from research already reviewed.

Workaround to investigate: Whether coordinators compensate with calls, group messages, or spreadsheets. The scenario does not establish which manual workaround they actually use; we need to confirm that before presenting one as fact.

4. Problem hook

We must protect RouteLogic's largest enterprise account from switching to a leaner competitor by addressing the administrative friction that slows frontline fleet coordination.

5. Value proposition

For frontline fleet coordinators, RouteLogic Velocity will make urgent coordination tasks simpler and faster while keeping advanced enterprise capabilities available, because declining usage and our largest account's competitor trial make restoring operational usefulness an immediate priority.

Working assumption: A focused path to essential tasks can improve operational usefulness without removing capabilities needed by administrators and enterprise buyers. Discovery and prototype testing will determine which steps and features should change.

6. Cold-read self-review

Question

My answer

Is the business risk high-stakes enough to justify a new initiative?

Yes. The largest enterprise account is trialing a simpler competitor, and frontline usage is declining. This justifies investigating operational friction now; the revenue exposure still needs to be quantified.

Is the moment of misery systemic, or just an edge case?

The decline in frontline usage suggests a broader problem than one isolated complaint. However, we still need research and task-level evidence to identify which users and workflows are affected most.

Does the value proposition actually remove the obstacle?

It targets the stated obstacle: complexity that slows daily operations. We must validate whether a focused workflow improves correct task completion and completion time while preserving access to enterprise capabilities.
