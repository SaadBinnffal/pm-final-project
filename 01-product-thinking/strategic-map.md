Module 1 · Exercise 1 · Water-tracking app

Responses

Feature with no strategic answer: why is this a 12-week priority?

Friend leaderboard. I cannot justify prioritizing it within the next 12 weeks because we have no validated evidence that social competition addresses a specific user frustration or advances a business goal. Before committing engineering time, I would investigate the audience and its core tracking problems.

Feature that looks "correct" but has zero established strategic weight

Achievement badges. Badges look appropriate for a habit-tracking app, but the map provides no evidence that users asked for them, that they improve meaningful repeat use, or that they support a credible revenue path. Placing them under "motivation" organizes the idea; it does not establish its strategic value.

V3 vs. the Step 1 baseline: what changed, and was it PM knowledge that forced usefulness?

V1 is a broad feature list without a defined audience, validated problem, business objective, or prioritization. V3 focuses on busy adults aged 25–40 who forget to drink. It links ideas to jobs-to-be-done and friction, ranks candidates using preliminary effort / impact estimates, and exposes monetization trade-offs, retention risks, and strategic tensions.

For example, V3 makes the conflict between helpful reminders and notification fatigue explicit. It also asks how much value should remain free while preserving a reason to upgrade. These are decisions to investigate rather than a longer list of features to build.

V3 is more useful as a discovery framework because the PM context makes the options relevant and the trade-offs visible. It does not prove that the features or rankings will work: user needs, impact, effort, and willingness to pay still need validation. The improvement comes from the added context and constraints, not evidence that the model itself became smarter.

Key takeaway

The PM frames the problem, defines the audience, judges priorities and trade-offs, and checks the evidence. AI accelerates brainstorming and organization, while the PM remains accountable for deciding what deserves the team's effort.

One-line takeaway
